668|Letter names and how to pronounce them
669|The German alphabet
670|German uses the same letters as English (26 letters) <b>+ 4 extra letters</b>: <span class='de'>ä ö ü ß</span><br>The best part: German is pronounced almost exactly as it is written, so once you learn the letter sounds you can read any word.
671|Letters A to G
672|<span class='de'>A B C D E F G</span><br>In German: ah, bay, tsay, day, ay, eff, gay.<br>Watch out: <span class='de'>C</span> is called "tsay" and <span class='de'>G</span> is called "gay".
673|H to N
674|<span class='de'>H I J K L M N</span><br>hah, ee, yot, kah, ell, emm, enn.<br>The letter <span class='de'>J</span> is called "yot" and sounds like English y: <span class='de'>ja</span> = yes.
675|O to U
676|<span class='de'>O P Q R S T U</span><br>oh, pay, koo, err, ess, tay, oo.<br>The letter <span class='de'>R</span> is made in the throat, and is light at the start of a word.
677|V to Z
678|<span class='de'>V W X Y Z</span><br>fow, vay, iks, ypsilon, tset.<br>The biggest trick: <span class='de'>W</span> sounds like English V, and <span class='de'>V</span> usually sounds like F!
679|The extra letters
680|<span class='de'>Ä Ö Ü ß</span><br>ä like "e" in "bed" (long): <span class='de'>Äpfel</span><br>ö round your lips: <span class='de'>Öl</span><br>ü like "ee" with rounded lips: <span class='de'>über</span><br>ß a double s, like ss: <span class='de'>Straße</span>
