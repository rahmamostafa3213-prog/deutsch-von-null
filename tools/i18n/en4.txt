760|The secret of the German artikel
761|Why three articles?
762|English has only "the", but German has 3:<br><span class='de'>der</span> for masculine, <span class='de'>die</span> for feminine, <span class='de'>das</span> for neuter.<br>There is not always a logical rule. The secret is to <b>learn each word with its article</b> from day one.
763|der: masculine
764|Men, masculine professions, days of the week and months:<br><span class='de'>der Vater</span> father, <span class='de'>der Montag</span> Monday, <span class='de'>der Tisch</span> table
765|die: feminine
766|Women, feminine professions and most words ending in -e:<br><span class='de'>die Mutter</span> mother, <span class='de'>die Lampe</span> lamp, <span class='de'>die Küche</span> kitchen
767|das: neuter
768|Mostly things, and words ending in -chen:<br><span class='de'>das Haus</span> house, <span class='de'>das Kind</span> child, <span class='de'>das Wasser</span> water
769|And the plural? Easy!
770|The plural is always <span class='de'>die</span> whatever the singular is:<br>der Tisch → die Tische<br>das Haus → die Häuser
771|Mother is feminine: die Mutter.
772|House is neuter: das Haus.
773|Table is masculine: der Tisch.
774|The plural of der Apfel is die Äpfel. In German the plural article is always:
775|The plural is always die.
